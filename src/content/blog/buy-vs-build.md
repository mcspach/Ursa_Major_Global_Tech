For fifteen years the advice was automatic: never build what you can rent.
Custom software cost six figures and a year; a SaaS subscription cost $99 a
month. Easy call.

That math has quietly broken, and most teams haven't re-run it.

## What actually changed

AI-assisted development didn't make software free — but it collapsed the cost
of a specific category: **internal tools with well-understood requirements.**
The client portal, the quoting calculator, the inventory dashboard, the
review-collection widget. The things where you're paying a SaaS vendor $500 to
$3,000 a month to use 20% of their feature set.

That category used to cost $80k to build. Today, built by a team that knows
its way around modern tooling, it's often $10–20k. Against a $1,500/month
subscription stack, the payback window is under a year — and then you own the
asset.

## The new decision framework

Rent when:

- **The problem is genuinely hard.** Payments infrastructure, email
  deliverability, video calls — Stripe and friends earn their fees. Don't
  build these. Ever.
- **The tool is your system of record for compliance.** Accounting and payroll
  stay rented.
- **You use most of what you pay for.** If the fit is good, renting is fine.

Consider building when:

- **You're paying for a fraction of the features** and the fraction you use is
  simple CRUD with a workflow around it
- **The tool touches your differentiation** — if your process *is* your edge,
  renting the generic version sands the edge off
- **Per-seat pricing scales against you** — the subscription grows with
  headcount; owned software doesn't
- **You've stacked three tools plus Zapier** to approximate one workflow

## The catch nobody mentions

Owned software needs an owner. Budget roughly 15–20% of build cost annually
for upkeep, or have a partner on retainer. "We built it and nobody maintained
it" is how you end up worse off than renting.

## Run the numbers

Total your SaaS spend on tools where you use a fifth of the features. Multiply
by three years. If that number is bigger than a build quote — and in 2026 it
often is — the automatic answer isn't automatic anymore.
